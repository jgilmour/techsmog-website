PermitReady vs RoadReady

Two apps for logging supervised driving hours, compared honestly — including the parts where the other one wins.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Compare › PermitReady vs RoadReady

PermitReady is an independent app not affiliated with any DMV or state agency. RoadReady is referenced by name in official materials in Massachusetts, Wisconsin, Ohio, and Montana — if you're in one of those states and want to follow official instructions exactly, that's worth knowing before reading further.

We make PermitReady. We have tried to write this page so that a reader in Massachusetts who should stay with RoadReady finishes it knowing that.

Which should you use?

Use RoadReady if…

  • You're in Ohio — use RoadReady Ohio specifically, through RoadReady.ohio.gov
  • Your state's official form or instructions name it and you want the path of least resistance
  • You're on Android
  • You want automatic telematics logging rather than typing in each drive
  • You want a parent dashboard that updates on its own
  • Your state expects a PDF affidavit the app can generate

Use PermitReady if…

  • You also need permit-test practice — all 50 states plus DC, in the same app
  • You don't want accounts or a second phone involved
  • You need it to work offline, with no signal and no sync
  • Your current app has stopped working and you need your hours somewhere reliable
  • You'd rather not grant background location to a teenager's phone

Using both is also a legitimate answer: the state-referenced app for the paperwork, PermitReady for the studying and an offline copy of the hours.

What RoadReady does better

Genuinely, and not as a setup for a rebuttal:

  • Automatic telematics logging. The app can detect and record a drive without anyone remembering to open it. For families where the logging keeps getting forgotten, this is the single most valuable feature either app has, and PermitReady does not have it.
  • Android support. RoadReady runs on both platforms. PermitReady does not run on Android at all, and no amount of other features fixes that if you're on a Pixel.
  • In-app parent view. A supervising parent sees the log update in their own app. No file to send, no import step, no "did you get it?"
  • State recognition in Massachusetts, Wisconsin, Ohio, and Montana. Being named in official materials removes ambiguity about whether your record will be accepted. That is worth real money in avoided hassle at the counter.
  • PDF affidavit export in some states. The app can produce the certification document the state expects, rather than leaving you to transcribe totals onto a form.
  • An established track record. Safe Roads Alliance reports the program has been used by millions of families, and RoadReady has been recognized with a U.S. Department of Transportation award. It has been doing this far longer than we have.

What PermitReady does better

  • No account required. No sign-up, no email verification, no password to reset, and nothing that can lock you out of your own record.
  • No second phone. One device, one app. A parent who wants a copy gets a file; a parent who wants their own running log can import it, but nothing breaks if they never install anything.
  • Works fully offline. Every question bank and every logged drive lives on the device. No signal, no sync, no server between you and your own hours.
  • Studying and logging in one app. RoadReady does not do permit-test practice; PermitReady covers all 50 states plus DC with handbook-based questions, then keeps going with the log after you pass. That is the whole reason the app exists.
  • Simpler manual entry. Date, duration, conditions, notes — a drive takes about fifteen seconds to log, with no pairing state, no sync status, and no account role to get wrong.
  • No "Always Allow" location requirement. PermitReady never requests location permission, because it does not track where you drive. For some parents that is a feature in itself.

Frequently asked

Does my state require the RoadReady app?

No state requires a specific app. Massachusetts, Wisconsin, Ohio, and Montana reference RoadReady by name in official materials, which makes it the smoothest option there. What states require is an accurate record of hours and, usually, a parent or guardian certification. Confirm the accepted format with your own licensing agency.

Can PermitReady log my drives automatically?

No. Every drive is entered by hand. The app does not use GPS and never asks for location permission. That is a deliberate trade — it is why the log works with no signal and no permissions — but if you want automatic detection, RoadReady has it and we do not.

Is PermitReady on Android?

No. iPhone and iPad only. If the teen or the supervising parent is on Android, RoadReady is the one that will work for both of you.

Can my parent see my log without installing anything?

Yes. You send them a file over AirDrop, Messages, or Mail. They can read it, keep it, or import it into their own copy of PermitReady if they want a running total of their own. There is no account for either of you.

Will PermitReady's log be accepted by my DMV?

PermitReady's log is an accurate record you can copy onto whatever form your state requires. No state has formally approved or rejected it as a substitute for an official form. Always confirm with your state agency what log format they accept.

Which app is cheaper?

RoadReady is free. PermitReady is free with ads, with an optional one-time $4.99 purchase that removes ads and the advertising SDK entirely. Neither app has a subscription.
