RoadReady Giving You Trouble? Here's What to Do

The fixes worth trying first, why the problem keeps coming back — and how to move your hours somewhere no account can lock you out of.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Help › Switching from RoadReady

If RoadReady keeps crashing, won't load your hours, or is giving you trouble — here's what's going on, what to try, and what to do if trying doesn't work. The quick fixes are first, because most problems are fixable. If yours isn't, the second half of this page is about getting your hours somewhere they cannot be locked away from you.

Before you do anything else: if you can currently see your logged hours anywhere — on the teen's device, on the parent's device, in an old emailed report — export or screenshot them now. RoadReady stores your log on its servers rather than on your phone, so a working account today is not a guarantee of a readable log tomorrow. Do not uninstall an app that holds the only copy of your record.

First, the fixes that usually work

Genuinely try these. Most RoadReady problems are ordinary app problems, and it would be a waste of your evening to switch apps over something a password reset would have solved.

  • Update the app. RoadReady shipped nine releases in roughly seven weeks between June and August 2026, most labeled as stability improvements. The version you are on may simply predate the fix. Check the App Store or Play Store manually rather than assuming automatic updates ran.
  • Reset the password instead of retyping it. If your account was migrated, a saved password may no longer be the live one. A reset writes a fresh credential into the current system.
  • Check which account you're signing into. RoadReady pairs a teen account with a parent account, usually under two different email addresses. Signing in with the wrong one looks exactly like a rejected password.
  • Check the other device in the pair. If hours are missing on one side, they are often still visible on the other. This is the single most useful check when a total looks wrong.
  • Force quit, reopen, restart the phone. Boring, but it clears a stuck session often enough to be worth thirty seconds.
  • Check permissions if drives aren't logging automatically. Location set to Always with Precise Location on, Background App Refresh enabled, Low Power Mode off, and battery optimisation disabled for the app on Android. Automatic detection also needs a data connection to sync.
  • Reinstall — but only after you have a copy of your hours. The log lives on RoadReady's servers, so a reinstall does not destroy it. It also does not fix an account that is itself broken.
  • Contact Safe Roads Alliance support. A server-side account cannot be repaired from your phone, and no third party can do it for you. We can't — we have no access to RoadReady accounts of any kind.

When RoadReady is genuinely the better choice

Not everyone reading this should switch, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

  • Massachusetts, Wisconsin, Ohio, and Montana reference RoadReady by name in official materials. Ohio runs its program through RoadReady.ohio.gov with BMV integration. Being named in state materials removes any argument at the counter about whether your record is acceptable, and that is worth real hassle avoided.
  • You're on Android. PermitReady is iPhone and iPad only. No feature list fixes that.
  • You want automatic drive detection. RoadReady's telematics logs a drive without anyone remembering to open an app. For families where the logging keeps getting forgotten, that is the most valuable feature either app has, and PermitReady does not have it.
  • Your state expects a PDF affidavit the app can generate. RoadReady produces one in some states. PermitReady does not produce any state form.
  • You want a parent dashboard that updates on its own. PermitReady shares a file; it does not sync.

If any of those apply, the better move is usually to keep working with Safe Roads Alliance support until the account is right — and, if you like, keep an offline copy of your hours in PermitReady as insurance. Using both is a perfectly reasonable answer.

Moving your hours to PermitReady

If you would rather not depend on an account at all, here is the whole process. It takes about twenty minutes.

Step 1 — Get your hours out of RoadReady

Try these in order, and stop as soon as one gives you a copy you can read outside the app.

  • Look for a PDF or report export. RoadReady has historically offered a printable summary of logged drives, and in some states a PDF affidavit. Check the log or summary screen for share, export, print, or "email report". If it exists, send it to yourself.
  • Look for a CSV export. Easiest format to re-enter from — one row per drive.
  • Check the parent account too. If the teen account is stuck, the parent account may still see and export the same drives, or the reverse.
  • Screenshot everything. If you cannot get a file out, scroll the drive list and screenshot it. Screenshots of your own log are a perfectly good source for re-entry.

If no export is available at all — the situation some locked-out users describe — you are down to manual reconstruction, which is less dire than it sounds, because what your state asks for is a total and usually a night subtotal, not a forensic record of every trip. Gather any running total you screenshotted before the trouble started, your supervising parent's recollection of the drives they were in the car for, and any paper log or notes kept alongside the app. Be honest about the number: the hours exist to make a safer driver, and the parent signature at the end is an attestation.

Step 3 — Add your existing hours

Each entry has a date (backdate freely — this is what makes re-entry possible), a duration, condition tags (highway, night, rain, city streets, parking, heavy traffic — tag everything that applied, and be careful with night, since most states count it separately), and optional notes.

  • With a full export, enter drives one for one. Tedious, but roughly a minute per ten drives once you find a rhythm, and the result matches your old record exactly.
  • With only totals, enter consolidated entries — "March 2026, 12 hours, city streets" plus a separate night entry — and put the source in the notes: "carried over from previous log app, exported 6 Aug 2026". A note explaining where a number came from is far better than a suspiciously round total with no explanation.

The running total rebuilds itself as you go. If you are starting a log from scratch rather than moving one, how to log driving hours covers what to record each session.

Step 4 — Verify against your state

  • Use the supervised hours calculator to see how many total and night hours are left.
  • Check the state-by-state hours table and the night-hours table for your specific numbers.
  • Confirm the log format your state accepts. Some want a specific affidavit signed by a parent; some accept any accurate record. PermitReady's log is a record you copy from — it is not a state form, and no state has approved or rejected it as a substitute for one.

Why PermitReady doesn't have these problems

Not because it is better engineered — because it is built differently, and the differences remove whole categories of failure:

  • No account. No sign-up, no migration, no password reset, nothing that can lock you out of your own record. There is no login screen to loop on.
  • No server. Your log is stored on your device with Apple's SwiftData. We cannot see it, cannot lose it, and cannot break it with an update to a system you do not control.
  • Fully offline. No sync, no data connection, no "drive not recorded because you were in a dead zone".
  • No second phone. One device, one app. A parent who wants a copy gets a file over AirDrop, Messages, or Mail; a parent who wants their own running total can import it, with a preview that prevents double-counting.
  • Permit test practice in the same app — all 50 states plus DC, from the official handbooks.

The honest trade-off, in full: no Android version, no automatic drive detection or GPS (every drive is typed in, which is why no location permission is ever requested), no PDF affidavit or state form generation, no in-app parent dashboard or live sync, and no state recognition — PermitReady is not named in any state's official materials. If those matter more than the account-free, offline design, stay where you are.

Frequently asked questions

Why does RoadReady keep crashing or logging me out?

The most commonly reported cause in 2026 is the account migration that came with the 3.4 update — RoadReady's own homepage carried a banner about migration difficulties as of August 2026. Update to the latest version first, then use a password reset rather than a saved password, and confirm you are signing into the right account of the pair. If the account itself is broken, only Safe Roads Alliance can fix it; it is a server-side record, not something on your phone.

Will I lose my hours if I switch?

Only if you delete your old app before getting a copy out. RoadReady stores your log on its servers, not on your device, so an uninstalled app plus an inaccessible account can mean an unreadable record. Export or screenshot first, then re-enter. Backdating is fully supported in PermitReady, so a rebuilt log reads the same as the original.

Can I use both apps at once?

Yes, and in Massachusetts, Wisconsin, Ohio, and Montana it is often the sensible answer: keep the state-referenced app for the paperwork, and use PermitReady for the permit-test practice and an offline copy of the hours that no account can lock you out of.

Does my state require the RoadReady app?

No state requires a specific app to log supervised driving hours. Massachusetts, Wisconsin, Ohio, and Montana reference RoadReady by name in official materials, which makes it the path of least resistance there. What states actually require is an accurate record and, in most cases, a parent-signed certification. Confirm the accepted format with your own licensing agency.

Is a re-entered log still valid?

A log is a record you keep, and its value is its accuracy. Re-entering drives from an export or from screenshots preserves that accuracy. Put the source in the notes field — "carried over from previous log app, exported 6 Aug 2026" — and never inflate a number you cannot stand behind. Most states have a parent sign a sworn certification of the hours.

What can't PermitReady do?

No Android version, no automatic drive detection or GPS, no PDF affidavit generation, no in-app parent dashboard, and no state recognition. Those are real limitations and they are listed in full on this page. What you get instead is no account, no second phone, and a log that works with no signal.
