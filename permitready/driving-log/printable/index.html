A printable driving log does three jobs: it is a backup when an app or a phone fails, it is a paper record a parent can sign, and in some states it is the required document. Below are links to each state's official form where one exists, plus a generic template you can print in one click.

Print this log

Use this if your state does not publish a form of its own, or as a running record you copy onto the official form later. Print one sheet per fifteen drives and keep it in the glovebox.

Which states publish an official form

States fall into three groups, and knowing which one you are in saves an argument at the counter:

  • States with a mandatory or officially published form. Massachusetts (RMV Supervised Driving Log PDF) and New York (form MV-262) both publish documents you are expected to turn up with. Texas and Florida provide log forms through their agency sites and driver education providers. Use the state's document.
  • States that reference a specific app. Massachusetts and Wisconsin reference RoadReady in official materials; Ohio operates its own state-built app, RoadReady Ohio; Montana's state log PDF names RoadReady as an option. In those states, following the state's instruction is the shortest path.
  • States with no required format. California is the clearest example: no mandatory form, the parent certifies the hours on the license application, and the DMV simply recommends keeping a log. Any accurate record works.

What a driving log needs to contain

  • Date of each drive. Not just a weekly total — individual dated entries are what a reviewer expects.
  • Duration, or start and end times you can subtract.
  • Day or night, tracked separately, because almost every state sets a night minimum and it is the one families run short of.
  • Conditions — highway, city, rain, heavy traffic. A few states require specific condition hours (Pennsylvania wants 5 in bad weather, South Dakota wants 10 in inclement weather).
  • Supervisor identification, and usually a signature.
  • A running total and a parent or guardian certification at the end.

Paper, app, or both

Paper never runs out of battery, never loses an account, and is already in the format most agencies want. Its failure mode is that it lives in a glovebox and gets lost, and that nobody adds it up until the week before the road test.

Frequently asked questions

Does my state have an official printable driving log?

Some do. Massachusetts publishes a log PDF, New York uses MV-262, and Texas and Florida provide forms through their agency sites. California requires no specific form. For the rest, check the licensing agency's website using the table above.

Can I use a generic log instead of the state form?

Only where the state does not mandate a form. Where one exists, use it — a generic log is still valuable as the running record you fill the official form in from.

What has to be on the log?

Date, duration, day or night, conditions, supervisor, signature, and a running total. Anything less and you are asking a clerk to take your word for it.

Do I still need paper if I use an app?

Not always, but printing a copy at the end costs nothing and removes a failure point. Several states require a signed paper certification regardless of how the hours were tracked.

How many hours will I need?

Most states require 40 to 65 hours, with about half at 50. See the full state-by-state guide or run your own numbers in the hours calculator.
