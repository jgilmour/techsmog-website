How to use the numbers

  • Night hours count toward your total. A 50-hour requirement with a 10-hour night minimum means 50 hours total, at least 10 of them after dark — not 60.
  • Check what your state means by "night." Many states define it as beginning 30 minutes after sunset and ending 30 minutes before sunrise; others use fixed clock times. A drive that ends at dusk may not count.
  • Driver education often reduces the requirement. Texas drops to 10 hours with a driver education course, and Massachusetts reduces its total for teens who complete an approved driver skills program. Several other states have similar provisions.
  • Some states cap how much counts per day. Ohio, for example, limits how many hours can be credited in a single day, so a marathon Saturday won't clear your requirement in one go.
  • A parent usually has to sign. In most states the licensing office collects a signed certification of hours rather than the log itself. The log is what you fill that form in from.

Frequently asked

Do night hours count toward my total?

Yes, in nearly every state. Night hours are a subset of the total, not an addition to it. This calculator treats them that way.

What counts as night driving?

Usually a window defined relative to sunset and sunrise — commonly from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise. Some states use fixed times instead. Check your state's handbook for its exact definition.

Does the DMV actually check the log?

In most states a parent or guardian signs a certification of total hours, and that form is what the office collects. The log usually is not audited line by line, though some offices ask to see it and some states require specific breakdowns.

Why does this calculator say "approximate"?

Because hour requirements change, and because many states have exemptions and reductions tied to driver education, age, or program completion that a dropdown can't capture. Treat the result as a planning figure and verify with your licensing agency.
