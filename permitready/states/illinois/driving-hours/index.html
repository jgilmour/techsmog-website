Illinois Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night, plus nine months on the permit and a driver education course.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Illinois › Supervised Driving Hours

Illinois requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can move from an instruction permit to an initial license. The permit has to be held for nine months — longer than the six months most states use — and drivers under 18 must complete an approved driver education course (30 hours classroom plus 6 hours behind the wheel). A parent or guardian certifies the 50 hours. Illinois publishes a practice driving log parents can use to keep the record.

Who can supervise practice driving in Illinois

Illinois adds an experience requirement on top of the age floor, which rules out newly licensed 21-year-olds.

  • At least 21 years old, licensed, with a minimum of one year of driving experience.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • A certified driving instructor qualifies during a lesson.
  • The permit holder cannot drive between 10 PM and 6 AM on Sunday through Thursday, or 11 PM and 6 AM Friday and Saturday — the same curfew that applies to newly licensed teens. Plan night hours in the evening, not late at night.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night, and Illinois makes this slightly tricky by also imposing a nighttime curfew on permit holders: no driving after 10 PM Sunday through Thursday, or after 11 PM on Friday and Saturday. So the practical night window is between sunset and 10 PM. In Chicago that window is generous from October through March, when it is fully dark by 5:15 PM, and very tight in June, when sunset is after 8:20 PM and you have barely ninety minutes. If your permit period runs through a Midwest winter, get the night hours done then. Ten hours is roughly twenty half-hour evening drives.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Snow and ice hours are worth logging separately. Illinois does not require them, but a driver who has never handled a slushy Kennedy Expressway or a rural county road in a whiteout is missing the skill that matters most here.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the nine-month permit period is a little over an hour a week — the schedule is not the problem in Illinois. The problem is that nine months is long enough for families to forget to log, and the certification happens at a Driver Services facility where nobody is going to help you reconstruct it. Write the drive down the day it happens. If you fall behind, backfilling from calendar entries and text messages is possible but grim.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Illinois require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, before a driver under 18 can get an initial license. A parent or guardian certifies the total.

How long do you have to hold an Illinois permit?

Nine months. That is longer than the six months most states require, and it applies regardless of how quickly the 50 hours are finished.

How many night hours does Illinois require?

10 of the 50. Because permit holders cannot drive after 10 PM on weeknights or 11 PM on weekends, the practical night window is between sunset and curfew — which is much wider in winter than in summer.

Does Illinois have an official driving log form?

The Secretary of State publishes a 50-hour practice driving log for families. The certification itself is made by a parent or guardian at a Driver Services facility.

Is driver education required in Illinois?

Yes, for drivers under 18: 30 hours of classroom instruction plus 6 hours of behind-the-wheel training with an instructor. Those 6 hours do not count toward the 50 supervised hours.
