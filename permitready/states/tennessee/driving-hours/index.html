Tennessee Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night, certified by a parent, after 180 days on the learner permit.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Tennessee › Supervised Driving Hours

Tennessee requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can move from a learner permit to an intermediate restricted license. The learner permit has to be held for at least 180 days, and the teen has to be 16. A parent or guardian signs a certification of the hours, which is presented when applying. Tennessee does not require driver education, which makes the parent-supervised hours the entire training program for most teens here.

Who can supervise practice driving in Tennessee

Because Tennessee does not require driver education, the adult in the passenger seat is doing all of the teaching.

  • A licensed driver at least 21 years old in the front passenger seat.
  • A licensed driving instructor qualifies during a paid lesson, though the state does not require one.
  • No driver education requirement means no professional backstop. If nobody teaches highway merging deliberately, it does not get taught. Plan the curriculum, not just the hours.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night. The Department of Safety does not publish a rigid clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. Nashville and Memphis are dark by about 4:40 PM in December, so November through February is the efficient window. Tennessee night conditions worth practicing on purpose: mountain and ridge roads in East Tennessee with tight curves and no lighting, fog in the river valleys that appears at dusk, and interstate stretches where the lighting stops abruptly at the county line.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Build a curriculum, not just a total. With no required driver education in Tennessee, the 50 hours are the whole course — make sure they include highway merging, four-lane left turns, parallel parking, and rain.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the 180-day permit period is about two hours a week. Since Tennessee does not require a driver education course, the smartest use of those hours is structured: pick a skill for each drive rather than just accumulating time. A teen who has done 50 unstructured hours around their own neighborhood has met the requirement and learned less than one who did 30 varied ones.

How many supervised driving hours does Tennessee require?

Is driver education required in Tennessee?

No. Tennessee does not mandate a driver education course for teen licensing, which means the 50 supervised hours are the entire training program for most teens.

How long do you have to hold a Tennessee learner permit?

180 days, and the teen must be at least 16 to move to an intermediate restricted license.

How many night hours does Tennessee require?

10 of the 50. Treat night as actual darkness between sunset and sunrise. Winter evenings are the efficient way to bank them.
