West Virginia Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night, certified by a parent, before the Level 2 intermediate license.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › West Virginia › Supervised Driving Hours

West Virginia requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can move from a Level 1 instruction permit to a Level 2 intermediate license. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. The Level 1 permit is available at 15 and has to be held for six months (shorter for teens who complete an approved driver education course — confirm the current period with the DMV). West Virginia's mountain roads make the night and weather practice more valuable here than the bare numbers suggest.

Who can supervise practice driving in West Virginia

Standard supervision rule — the terrain is what makes West Virginia practice different from the state next door.

  • A licensed driver at least 21 years old in the front passenger seat.
  • A licensed driving instructor qualifies during a lesson.
  • Level 1 carries driving restrictions, including a nighttime limit. Plan night hours in the evening and check the current rules before assuming a late drive is allowed.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night. The DMV does not publish a rigid clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. Charleston and Morgantown are dark by about 5:10 PM in December, so late fall through winter is the efficient window. West Virginia's terrain makes night practice unusually important: two-lane mountain roads with switchbacks, no shoulder, no lighting, and a rock face on one side and a drop on the other behave nothing like a lit four-lane. Add fog, which settles in the valleys at dusk and can cut visibility to a few car lengths, and coal truck traffic on narrow roads.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Mountain grades deserve deliberate practice. Engine braking on a long descent is a skill, not an instinct, and it is not going to come up on a flat suburban loop.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the six-month Level 1 period is about two hours a week. In West Virginia, a trip to a larger town can eat an hour of that in one go — useful, but make sure the mix includes town driving, four-way stops, parking, and merging, not just curvy two-lanes. The hours are easy to reach; the variety takes intention.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does West Virginia require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, before a Level 2 intermediate license. A parent or guardian certifies the total.

How long do you have to hold a West Virginia Level 1 permit?

Six months, and completing an approved driver education course can shorten that. Confirm the current periods with the DMV.

How many night hours does West Virginia require?

10 of the 50. Treat night as actual darkness, and get some of those hours on mountain two-lanes rather than only in town, since that is where the real difficulty is.

Does driver education replace the 50 hours in West Virginia?

No. An approved course can shorten the permit holding period, but the supervised hours still have to be completed and certified.

Does West Virginia have an official driving log form?

West Virginia requires certification of the 50 hours signed by a parent or guardian, submitted with the Level 2 application. Get the current form from the DMV.
