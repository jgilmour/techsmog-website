Alaska Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

40 hours of supervised driving, at least 10 of them in progressively more challenging conditions — which in Alaska means dark, ice, or both.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Alaska › Supervised Driving Hours

Alaska requires 40 hours of supervised driving before a teen can move from an instruction permit to a provisional license, and at least 10 of those hours must be in progressively challenging conditions. Alaska is unusual in how it words that: instead of a flat night minimum, the DMV asks for experience in harder conditions, which in practice means darkness, snow, ice, and poor visibility. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Alaska also has one of the youngest permit ages in the country at 14.

Who can supervise practice driving in Alaska

Alaska adds an experience requirement on top of the age floor, which trips people up when an older sibling or a recently licensed relative volunteers.

  • At least 21 years old and licensed, with a minimum of one year of driving experience.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the whole drive.
  • Alaska's permit age is 14, which means a lot of practice happens well before the teen is anywhere near the road test — the supervision rule does not relax because the permit holder is young.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Alaska frames the extra 10 hours as "progressively more challenging conditions" rather than a straight night count, and the DMV includes both darkness and adverse weather in that. If you live in Alaska this is either the easiest requirement in the country or the hardest, depending entirely on the month. In Fairbanks in December, almost every drive is a night drive and the 10 hours accumulate without trying. In June, actual darkness barely exists north of the Alaska Range, so the challenging-conditions hours have to come from rain, gravel, low sun glare, and unfamiliar road types. Plan the calendar around that: bank the hard-condition hours in the winter half of the permit period and use summer for volume.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Note the condition, not just the time. Because Alaska's requirement is about conditions rather than the clock, a log entry that says "ice, dark, 45 min" is worth more to you at the counter than one that just says "7 PM."

How to actually get to 40 hours

Forty hours over the six-month minimum permit period is a bit under two hours a week. Most Alaska families find the total is not the constraint — the daylight and the road conditions are. Start in the fall if you can, so the challenging-conditions hours land naturally, and use the long summer evenings for the bulk of the remaining time.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Alaska require?

40 hours, including at least 10 hours in progressively more challenging conditions, before a teen can apply for a provisional license. A parent or guardian certifies the total.

What counts as challenging conditions in Alaska?

The DMV's wording covers darkness and adverse weather. Snow, ice, rain, fog, gravel, and night driving all fit. Because of Alaska's daylight swings, most families bank these hours in the winter half of the permit period.

How old do you have to be to get an Alaska instruction permit?

14, which is one of the youngest permit ages in the country. The provisional license comes at 16, after at least six months on the permit and the 40 logged hours.

Does Alaska have an official driving log form?

Alaska does not publish a standalone log PDF that is in wide use. The parent or guardian certification is handled as part of the license application, so keep your own dated record and confirm the current form with the DMV.

Who can supervise practice driving in Alaska?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old with a minimum of one year of driving experience, seated in the front passenger seat.
