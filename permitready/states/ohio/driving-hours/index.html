Ohio Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours with 10 at night, certified on the Fifty-Hour Affidavit — and Ohio's BMV supports RoadReady as a digital log.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Ohio › Supervised Driving Hours

Ohio requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can take the road test. The hours are certified on the BMV's Fifty-Hour Affidavit (form BMV 5791), signed by a parent or guardian. Ohio also requires driver education for anyone under 18 — commonly 24 hours of classroom plus 8 hours behind the wheel — and the temporary instruction permit (TIPIC) has to be held for six months. Ohio is one of the few states with a BMV-integrated digital log: RoadReady is supported for recording the 50 hours.

Who can supervise practice driving in Ohio

Ohio's rule centers on the parent or guardian, with room for an instructor or another qualifying adult.

  • A licensed parent, guardian, or custodian, or a licensed driving instructor, in the front passenger seat.
  • Another licensed adult 21 or older may supervise — confirm the current wording with the BMV before relying on it.
  • Permit holders under 16 cannot drive between sunset and sunrise except with a parent or guardian, which shapes when night hours can happen.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night, and Ohio's affidavit means someone signs their name to that number. The BMV does not publish a strict clock definition, so use actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. In Columbus and Cleveland, December darkness starts around 5:05 PM, while June holds light past 9 PM, so the winter half of the permit period is much more efficient. Ohio-specific night conditions worth building in: lake-effect snow at night in the northeast, unlit rural county roads in the south and west, and the specific problem of a wet freeway at night where lane markings vanish under headlight glare.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Watch the daily limit. Ohio is one of the states where a per-day cap on logged hours is commonly applied, so a single eight-hour road trip may not count as eight hours. Confirm with the BMV.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the six-month TIPIC period is about two hours a week. Because Ohio caps how much can be logged in a day, the marathon-road-trip strategy does not work here even if it would be legal elsewhere — steady short drives are the only approach that adds up. Attach driving to the school run and one weekly errand and the total takes care of itself.

Tracking your hours

Ohio is a special case, and it is worth being straight about it. The Ohio BMV supports RoadReady as a digital driving log with BMV integration, so hours logged there are part of the state's own process rather than a private record you transcribe later. If you are logging Ohio hours, RoadReady or the paper Fifty-Hour Affidavit (BMV form 5791) is the record that counts. We are not going to suggest you keep your official hours somewhere else.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Ohio require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, certified on the BMV's Fifty-Hour Affidavit, form BMV 5791, signed by a parent or guardian.

What is the Ohio Fifty-Hour Affidavit?

BMV form 5791. It is the sworn statement a parent or guardian signs certifying that the teen completed 50 hours of supervised driving including 10 at night. It is submitted with the road test application.

Does Ohio support RoadReady?

Yes. The Ohio BMV supports RoadReady as a digital driving log with BMV integration, so hours recorded there are part of the state's process rather than a private record you have to transcribe.

Is there a daily limit on logged hours in Ohio?

A cap of around 4 hours per day is commonly applied, which means long road trips do not count in full. Confirm the current rule with the BMV before planning around it.

Is driver education required in Ohio?

Yes, for drivers under 18: commonly 24 hours of classroom instruction plus 8 hours behind the wheel. Those instructor hours are separate from the 50 supervised hours.
