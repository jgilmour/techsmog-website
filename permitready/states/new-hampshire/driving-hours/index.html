New Hampshire Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

40 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night — and New Hampshire does not issue a learner's permit to anyone under 18.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › New Hampshire › Supervised Driving Hours

New Hampshire requires 40 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can get a Youth Operator License. New Hampshire is genuinely unusual in one respect: it does not issue learner's permits to drivers under 18. A teen 15½ or older may practice with a licensed adult at least 25 years old who has at least three years of driving experience — no permit card required. Driver education is required for anyone under 18, and the 40 supervised hours are on top of the course's behind-the-wheel instruction.

Who can supervise practice driving in New Hampshire

Because there is no permit card, the supervising driver rule is the legal authority for the teen to be driving at all. Getting it wrong is not a paperwork problem.

  • At least 25 years old and licensed, with at least three years of driving experience.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • The teen must be at least 15½ to practice at all.
  • There is no permit to show an officer. The supervising adult's qualifications are what make the drive lawful, so a 22-year-old sibling in the passenger seat is not a technicality — it is an unlicensed driver on the road.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 40 hours have to be at night. The DMV does not publish a clock definition, so use actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. New Hampshire's winters make this easy: Concord is dark by about 4:15 PM in December, so the drive home from practice already counts. What is worth doing deliberately here: unlit two-lane roads through the woods, where moose and deer are a real hazard and the road disappears into the trees, and winter night driving where a plowed road refreezes after sunset.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Winter road conditions are the New Hampshire skill. The state does not require weather hours, but ice on a hill road in January is the condition a new driver here will meet first.

How to actually get to 40 hours

Forty hours is about two hours a week over a five-month stretch. Because there is no permit clock, New Hampshire families have more scheduling freedom than most — and less structure. The thing that keeps it moving is treating driver education's start date as the beginning of the 40 hours, then attaching driving to routine trips from that day on.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does New Hampshire require?

40 hours, including at least 10 at night, before a Youth Operator License. That is on top of the behind-the-wheel instruction in the required driver education course.

Does New Hampshire issue learner's permits?

Not to drivers under 18. A teen 15 and a half or older may practice with a licensed adult at least 25 years old who has at least three years of driving experience. There is no permit card for under-18 drivers.

Who can supervise practice driving in New Hampshire?

A licensed driver at least 25 years old with at least three years of driving experience, in the front passenger seat. Because there is no permit, this rule is what makes the drive lawful.

How many night hours does New Hampshire require?

10 of the 40. New Hampshire's early winter sunsets — around 4:15 PM in December — make those hours easy to collect from November through February.

Is driver education required in New Hampshire?

Yes, for drivers under 18. The course commonly includes 30 hours of classroom instruction, 10 hours behind the wheel, and 6 hours of observation, all separate from the 40 supervised hours.
