Pennsylvania Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

65 hours of supervised driving — 10 at night and 5 in poor weather — certified on PennDOT form DL-180C.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Pennsylvania › Supervised Driving Hours

Pennsylvania requires 65 hours of supervised driving before a teen can take the road test for a junior license, and it is specific about the mix: at least 10 hours at night and at least 5 hours in poor weather conditions. Pennsylvania is one of the only states that requires bad-weather practice by name. A parent or guardian certifies the hours on form DL-180C, the Parent or Guardian Certification Form. The learner's permit has to be held for six months, and the junior license is available at 16½.

Who can supervise practice driving in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania allows a slightly younger supervisor if they are a parent, guardian, or spouse, which helps families where the older adult does not drive.

  • A licensed driver at least 21 years old, or
  • a licensed spouse, parent, or guardian at least 18, in the front passenger seat.
  • Permit holders have a midnight to 5 AM driving restriction, so night practice happens in the evening.
  • The DL-180C is signed, not estimated. It asks for three separate numbers, which is much harder to fake convincingly than a single total.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Pennsylvania is one of the few states that asks for conditions rather than just hours: 10 hours at night and 5 hours in poor weather, both inside the 65. PennDOT does not publish a strict clock definition of night, so use actual darkness. Pennsylvania's winter helps — Philadelphia and Pittsburgh are both dark by about 4:50 PM in December. Poor weather is the piece families forget until spring: rain, snow, sleet, and fog all count, and you cannot schedule them. When it rains and the teen is available, go drive. Five hours of weather practice takes a whole season to accumulate if you only take the opportunities that happen to be convenient.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Poor-weather hours cannot be planned, only caught. When it is raining and you have twenty minutes, that is the drive to take — five hours of weather practice is the hardest part of Pennsylvania's requirement to backfill.

How to actually get to 65 hours

Sixty-five hours over the six-month permit period is about two and a half hours a week — more than most states and quite doable with a routine. The scarce resources are night and weather. Bank the 10 night hours in late fall and winter when it is dark by 5 PM, and treat every rainy evening as an opportunity rather than a reason to stay home. Families who wait until the last month find themselves needing five hours of rain in a dry May.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Pennsylvania require?

65 hours, including at least 10 at night and at least 5 in poor weather conditions, certified by a parent or guardian on form DL-180C.

What is form DL-180C?

PennDOT's Parent or Guardian Certification Form. It is where a parent certifies the 65 hours, the 10 night hours, and the 5 poor-weather hours, and it is submitted with the road test application.

What counts as poor weather in Pennsylvania?

Rain, snow, sleet, and fog all fit. PennDOT does not publish a narrow definition, so use judgment and log the conditions honestly. Five hours is harder to collect than it sounds because you cannot schedule weather.

How long do you have to hold a Pennsylvania learner's permit?

Six months before the road test. The junior driver's license is available at 16 and a half.

Who can supervise practice driving in Pennsylvania?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old, or a licensed spouse, parent, or guardian at least 18, seated in the front passenger seat.
