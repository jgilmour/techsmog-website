Alabama Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving certified by a parent — or an approved driver education course instead — before the Stage II restricted license.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Alabama › Supervised Driving Hours

Alabama's graduated licensing law expects 50 hours of supervised driving before a teen moves from the Stage I learner's license to the Stage II restricted license at 16. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Completing a state-approved driver education course is the alternative path in place of the 50-hour certification. Alabama does not publish a separate night-hours minimum, which is unusual — but that does not make night practice optional in any practical sense.

Who can supervise practice driving in Alabama

Alabama keeps the supervision rule short, which means the burden is on you to get it right the first time.

  • A licensed driver at least 21 years old must occupy the seat beside the permit holder for the entire drive.
  • A licensed or certified driving instructor also satisfies the requirement during a lesson.
  • The supervisor has to be in the front passenger seat — a licensed adult in the back seat does not count, and neither does a parent standing in the driveway.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Alabama does not publish a specific night-hours minimum the way most states do, so there is no number to hit and no official definition of "night" to work against. That is a low bar, not a good reason to skip it. Practically speaking, plan on at least 10 hours after dark anyway: headlight glare, judging distance at night, and unlit rural two-lanes are exactly the conditions new drivers handle worst, and they are the conditions Alabama's Stage II night restriction (midnight to 6 AM, with exceptions) exists because of. Tag night drives in your log as you go so you can see the balance, even though no one is going to ask you for the number.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Track night drives even though Alabama does not require it. If your family moves out of state mid-permit — which happens more than you would think — the next state almost certainly does require a night count, and reconstructing it from memory is miserable.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours across a six-month permit period works out to about two hours a week, or roughly twenty minutes of driving on five days out of seven. That is the school run and one weekend errand. Families who try to bank it in weekend blocks tend to stall out around hour 30 — the drives get long, everyone gets tense, and it stops happening. Short, frequent, boring drives are what actually accumulate.

Tracking your hours

Alabama does not hand you a form, which means the record you bring is whatever you built. A notebook in the glovebox is fine. So is a spreadsheet. The only thing that matters is that a parent or guardian can honestly certify the total when you get to the license office.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Alabama require?

50 hours is the figure commonly cited under Alabama's graduated driver license law, certified by a parent or guardian, before a teen moves to the Stage II restricted license at 16. Completing a state-approved driver education course is the alternative to the 50-hour certification. Confirm the current requirement with ALEA before you apply.

Does Alabama require night driving hours?

ALEA does not publish a separate night-hours minimum. That makes Alabama one of a small group of states with no night number to hit. Plan on night practice anyway — Stage II carries a midnight-to-6-AM driving restriction, and the hours after dark are where new drivers are weakest.

Does Alabama have an official driving log form?

No statewide log PDF is published. The parent or guardian certifies the supervised driving as part of the Stage II license application, so any honest, dated record you keep — paper, spreadsheet, or app — is what backs that certification up.

Who can supervise practice driving in Alabama?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old, seated beside the permit holder. A licensed driving instructor also qualifies during a lesson.

Can driver education replace the 50 hours in Alabama?

A state-approved driver education course is the alternative path to the Stage II license in place of the parent-certified 50 hours. It replaces the certification rather than reducing it. Check with ALEA which specific courses qualify before you pay for one.
