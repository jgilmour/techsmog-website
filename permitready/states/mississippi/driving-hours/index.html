Mississippi Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

Mississippi does not set an hour count. It uses a 12-month learner's permit period instead — which changes how you plan practice, not whether you need it.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Mississippi › Supervised Driving Hours

Mississippi is one of the few states that does not publish a specific number of supervised driving hours. The graduated licensing law works on time instead: a learner's permit at 15, held for 12 months, then an intermediate license at 16, then a full license. There is no state hour count to certify and no official log form. That is a real difference from most states, and it means the amount of practice a new Mississippi driver gets is entirely a family decision.

Who can supervise practice driving in Mississippi

The supervision rule still applies even without an hour count attached to it.

  • A licensed driver at least 21 years old in the front passenger seat whenever the permit holder is driving.
  • A licensed driving instructor qualifies during a lesson.
  • The 12-month clock is the gate. Finishing practice early does not move the date; the permit still has to be held a full year.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Mississippi publishes no night-hours minimum, so there is no number to hit and no official definition of night to work against. Borrow the national norm: 10 hours after dark, counted between sunset and sunrise. The reason nearly every other state landed on that figure is not paperwork — night crash rates for 16- and 17-year-olds run far above daytime rates, and Mississippi's rural highway network is exactly the environment where that shows up. Unlit two-lanes, no shoulder, deer, and oncoming high beams are worth meeting with a parent in the car.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Nobody will ask for the log, so it is for you. Its only job in Mississippi is to show the family honestly whether the teen has driven at night, in rain, on the interstate, and in traffic — or has done forty trips to the same place.

How to actually get to enough practice to actually be ready

With no state number, borrow one: 50 hours with 10 at night is the most common requirement in the country and a fair target. Spread across the 12-month permit period that is about an hour a week — genuinely light. The risk in a state with no requirement is arriving at 16 with a clean record, a valid permit, and fifteen hours of experience, which satisfies the law and does not make anyone ready for a Mississippi highway at night.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Mississippi require?

Mississippi does not publish a fixed statewide hour requirement. The graduated licensing law relies on a 12-month learner's permit period instead. Confirm current requirements with the Driver Service Bureau, since states do add hour requirements over time.

Does Mississippi require night driving hours?

No night minimum is published. Ten hours after dark is the national norm and a sensible personal target, particularly given how much rural highway driving Mississippi teens do.

How long do you have to hold a Mississippi learner's permit?

12 months before applying for an intermediate license at 16. Driver education may affect the timeline — confirm with the Driver Service Bureau.

Does Mississippi have an official driving log form?

No. There is no state supervised driving log form, because there is no hour count to certify.

Who can supervise practice driving in Mississippi?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old, seated in the front passenger seat.
