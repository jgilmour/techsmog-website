Michigan Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours with 10 at night for Level 2 — but 30 of those hours (with 2 at night) have to be done before Segment 2 driver education even starts.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Michigan › Supervised Driving Hours

Michigan requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can move to a Level 2 intermediate license. Michigan's structure is different from most states: driver education comes in two segments, and to enroll in Segment 2 a teen needs at least 30 hours of driving experience (including 2 at night) and three months on the Level 1 learner's license. So the 50 hours is not one long push — there is a checkpoint at 30. A parent or guardian certifies the hours.

Who can supervise practice driving in Michigan

Michigan is one of the states where a parent can delegate supervision, but the delegation has to be deliberate.

  • A licensed parent or guardian, or
  • a licensed adult 21 or older designated by the parent or guardian, in the front passenger seat.
  • A driver education instructor qualifies during Segment 1 and Segment 2 sessions.
  • Michigan's permit age is 14 years 8 months, the youngest starting point in the country after Alaska and Iowa. That is a very young driver on a Michigan winter road — the supervising adult's attention is doing real work.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night, and two of those have to happen early, before Segment 2 driver education. Michigan does not publish a clock definition, so read night as actual darkness. Michigan's geography makes this seasonal in a big way: in Detroit, December darkness starts around 5:05 PM, while in the western Upper Peninsula summer light lingers past 10 PM. Plan night hours for late fall through early spring. Michigan-specific night conditions worth seeking out: lake-effect snow at night, unlit rural roads with deer, and the specific misery of a slushy freeway where the lane lines have disappeared under salt spray.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Hit the 30-hour checkpoint on time. Segment 2 enrollment requires 30 hours including 2 at night plus three months at Level 1. Falling behind there delays everything downstream.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours across the six-month Level 1 period is about two hours a week, but the front-loaded 30-hour checkpoint means the first three months are heavier: roughly two and a half hours a week to be clear for Segment 2. Start immediately, use routine trips, and get the two early night hours done in the first few weeks rather than scrambling.

Tracking your hours

Michigan checks the hours twice — once at 30 for Segment 2 and again at 50 for Level 2 — so a running total that separates night hours is genuinely useful rather than just tidy.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Michigan require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, before a Level 2 intermediate license. A separate checkpoint requires 30 hours including 2 at night before a teen can enroll in Segment 2 driver education.

What is the 30-hour requirement in Michigan?

To enroll in Segment 2 driver education, a teen needs at least 30 hours of driving experience including 2 hours at night, plus three months on the Level 1 learner's license. Those 30 hours count toward the eventual 50.

How many night hours does Michigan require?

10 of the 50, with 2 of them needed early for Segment 2. Treat night as actual darkness, and plan around Michigan's long summer evenings by banking night hours between October and March.

How old do you have to be to get a Michigan Level 1 license?

14 years and 8 months, after completing Segment 1 driver education. It is one of the youngest starting points in the country.

Does Michigan have an official driving log form?

Driver education providers and the Secretary of State supply a driving log for the required hours. A parent or guardian certifies the totals when applying for Level 2.
