Nevada Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours with 10 at night on the Beginning Driver Experience Log — or 100 hours if driver education is not available near you.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Nevada › Supervised Driving Hours

Nevada requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, recorded on the DMV's Beginning Driver Experience Log. Nevada pairs that with a driver education requirement for teens under 18 — and it has an unusual fallback: if no driver education course is available within a reasonable distance of where you live, the requirement becomes 100 hours of supervised driving instead. That rule exists because of Nevada's rural counties, where the nearest course can be a long way off. A parent or guardian signs the log.

Who can supervise practice driving in Nevada

Nevada adds an experience requirement to the age floor, and the 100-hour rule is the state-specific thing to check before you start.

  • At least 21 years old, licensed, with at least one year of driving experience.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • Check which total applies to you first. Families in rural counties without a nearby driver education course are on the 100-hour track, and finding that out in month five is a bad way to learn it.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night (twenty if you are on the 100-hour track). Nevada does not publish a rigid clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. Las Vegas and Reno are dark by about 4:30 PM in December and hold light past 8 PM in June, so the winter half of the year is more efficient. Nevada-specific night driving worth practicing: the transition from the lit Strip or a lit interstate to a completely dark desert highway, where your eyes need real time to adjust and animals appear at the edge of the beam with no warning.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Confirm your total before you count. Fifty and 100 are very different targets, and the difference comes down to whether driver education was reasonably available to you.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the six-month permit period is about two hours a week. One hundred hours is four hours a week, which is a serious commitment and the main reason rural Nevada families push to get into a driver education course if one is reachable at all. Either way, the Beginning Driver Experience Log gets submitted, so the number needs to be real.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Nevada require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, recorded on the DMV's Beginning Driver Experience Log. If driver education is not reasonably available where you live, the requirement is 100 hours instead.

What is the Beginning Driver Experience Log?

Nevada's official supervised driving log. A parent or guardian signs it and it is submitted with the license application, so it needs to be filled in accurately rather than reconstructed.

Why do some Nevada teens need 100 hours?

Because driver education is required for drivers under 18 where it is available. In rural counties where no course is reasonably accessible, Nevada substitutes a 100-hour supervised driving requirement.

How many night hours does Nevada require?

10 of the 50. Treat night as actual darkness between sunset and sunrise, and get some of those hours away from city lighting where desert highways are genuinely dark.

Who can supervise practice driving in Nevada?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old with at least one year of driving experience, seated in the front passenger seat.
