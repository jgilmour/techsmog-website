Vermont Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

40 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night, plus driver education and a full year on the learner's permit.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Vermont › Supervised Driving Hours

Vermont requires 40 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can get a junior driver's license. Vermont also requires a driver education course for teen applicants and a learner's permit held for at least one year — among the longest waiting periods in the country. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. The 6 hours of behind-the-wheel instruction in driver education are separate from the 40 and do not count toward them.

Who can supervise practice driving in Vermont

Vermont's supervision rule is stricter than most for non-family adults.

  • A licensed parent, guardian, or driver education instructor, or
  • a licensed driver at least 25 years old, in the front passenger seat.
  • Confirm the current wording with the DMV before relying on a non-family supervisor — this is the rule most likely to have been updated since any guide you read.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 40 hours have to be at night. Vermont's winter does most of the work: Burlington is dark by about 4:15 PM in December, so the drive home from anything is a night drive from November through February. In June, light lasts past 8:45 PM. The conditions worth seeking out in Vermont after dark are specific: unlit dirt and gravel town roads, where the edge of the road is genuinely hard to find; frost heaves and ice on shaded curves; and the moose and deer that are a real and serious hazard on Route 100 and similar roads at night.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Dirt road and mud season practice matters here. Vermont does not require it, but a driver who has never handled a soft dirt road in April is missing something the road test will never check and April will.

How to actually get to 40 hours

Forty hours over the one-year permit period is well under an hour a week — one of the gentlest paces in the country. That is the trap: a year is long enough to forget the log entirely, and Vermont's requirement is easy enough that families put it off until the license appointment is booked. Write it down as you go and the year does the rest.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Vermont require?

40 hours, including at least 10 at night, before a junior driver's license. A parent or guardian certifies the total.

How long do you have to hold a Vermont learner's permit?

One year, which is among the longest permit periods in the country. The junior driver's license is available at 16.

Is driver education required in Vermont?

Yes, for teen applicants. The course includes behind-the-wheel instruction, commonly 6 hours, and those hours are separate from the 40 supervised hours.

Who can supervise practice driving in Vermont?

A licensed parent, guardian, or driver education instructor, or a licensed driver at least 25 years old, seated in the front passenger seat. Confirm the current rule with the DMV.

How many night hours does Vermont require?

10 of the 40. Vermont's early winter darkness makes November through February the easy window for collecting them.
