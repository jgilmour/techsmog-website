Kentucky Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

60 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night — one of the higher totals in the country — and 180 days on the permit.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Kentucky › Supervised Driving Hours

Kentucky requires 60 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can take the road test for an intermediate license. The permit has to be held for at least 180 days. Sixty hours puts Kentucky in the top handful of states by total required hours. A parent or guardian documents and signs off on the practice. Note that Kentucky driver licensing moved from Circuit Court Clerk offices to Transportation Cabinet regional licensing offices, so older instructions you find online may send you to the wrong building.

Who can supervise practice driving in Kentucky

The supervision rule is standard; the location of the office is the part that has changed recently.

  • A licensed driver at least 21 years old in the front passenger seat.
  • A licensed driving instructor qualifies during a lesson.
  • Go to a Transportation Cabinet regional driver licensing office, not the county Circuit Court Clerk. Licensing moved, and out-of-date guides still point at the courthouse.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 60 hours have to be at night. Kentucky's permit carries a midnight-to-6-AM restriction, so night practice happens in the evening, which is where it should happen anyway. The Transportation Cabinet does not publish a clock definition, so read night as actual darkness. In Louisville and Lexington, it is fully dark by about 6 PM from November through February — that is the window. What is worth seeking out in Kentucky specifically: unlit two-lane roads with hills and blind curves, where headlights come over a rise with no warning. Ten hours on lit city streets does not prepare anyone for that.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Sixty hours is a lot — log it as you go. Kentucky's total is 20 percent higher than the national norm, and reconstructing 60 hours from memory in month six is not a task anyone finishes honestly.

How to actually get to 60 hours

Sixty hours over the 180-day permit period works out to about two and a half hours a week. That is more than most states ask and it does require a routine: the school run each morning, one errand a week, one longer weekend drive. Families who wait until month four to get serious end up cramming, and cramming produces highway miles rather than the varied practice the road test actually checks.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Kentucky require?

60 hours, including at least 10 at night, before the road test for an intermediate license. That is one of the higher totals in the country.

How long do you have to hold a Kentucky permit?

180 days before taking the road test. The permit also carries a midnight to 6 AM driving restriction.

How many night hours does Kentucky require?

10 of the 60. Treat night as actual darkness between sunset and sunrise, and get some of those hours on unlit rural two-lanes rather than only in town.

Where do you go for a Kentucky driver's license now?

Kentucky driver licensing moved from Circuit Court Clerk offices to Transportation Cabinet regional driver licensing offices. Older online guides still point at the courthouse, which is no longer correct.

Does Kentucky have an official driving log form?

Kentucky requires documentation of the 60 hours signed by a parent or guardian and presented at the road test. Get the current form from a regional driver licensing office.
