New Jersey Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

New Jersey does not publish a logged hour count. It requires 6 hours of professional behind-the-wheel training and six months of supervised practice instead.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › New Jersey › Supervised Driving Hours

New Jersey's graduated driver licensing program is built around professional training and time rather than a parent-certified hour count. A 16-year-old with a Special Learner Permit must complete 6 hours of behind-the-wheel training with a licensed New Jersey driving school, and then practice under supervision for at least six months before taking the road test. The MVC does not publish a 50-hour log requirement the way most states do — you will see that figure on third-party sites, but the published New Jersey requirements are the training hours and the supervised period. Verify with the MVC before relying on any number.

Who can supervise practice driving in New Jersey

New Jersey's supervision rule is stricter than most, and the red decal requirement is the part visitors always ask about.

  • At least 21 years old, licensed, with at least three years of driving experience, in the front passenger seat.
  • Red decals are required. Under Kyleigh's Law, permit and probationary drivers under 21 must display reflective red decals on the front and rear license plates. New Jersey is the only state with this rule.
  • Permit restrictions apply during practice, including a nighttime driving restriction and limits on passengers. Check the current hours with the MVC.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

New Jersey does not publish a night-hours minimum, so there is no number to hit. There is, however, a nighttime restriction on permit and probationary drivers — commonly framed as no driving between 11:01 PM and 5 AM — which shapes when practice can happen. Use the evening window before the curfew. Ten hours after dark is the national norm and a reasonable personal target, and New Jersey is a state where it earns its keep: the Turnpike and Parkway at night, merging into fast traffic with poor lighting and heavy truck volume, is a different task from the same road at noon. Confirm the current curfew times with the MVC, since they have been adjusted before.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • No log is collected, so practice quality is the whole game. Six months is the requirement; what you do inside those six months is up to the family.

How to actually get to six months of real practice

Without a required number, borrow the national norm: 50 hours with 10 at night across the six-month supervised period, which is about two hours a week. New Jersey's driving environment argues for going past that — dense traffic, aggressive merges, jughandles, and circles are all things that take repetition. The 6 hours of professional instruction are a good foundation and nowhere near enough on their own.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does New Jersey require?

The MVC does not publish a parent-certified hour count. New Jersey requires 6 hours of behind-the-wheel training with a licensed driving school for Special Learner Permit holders, plus at least six months of supervised driving before the road test. Third-party sites often cite 50 hours; verify with the MVC.

What are the red decals in New Jersey?

Under Kyleigh's Law, permit and probationary drivers under 21 must display reflective red decals on the front and rear license plates. New Jersey is the only state that requires them.

Who can supervise practice driving in New Jersey?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old with at least three years of driving experience, seated in the front passenger seat.

Does New Jersey have a nighttime driving restriction for permit holders?

Yes. Permit and probationary drivers face a nighttime driving restriction, commonly stated as 11:01 PM to 5 AM. Confirm the current times with the MVC, since they have been adjusted in the past.

Does New Jersey have an official driving log form?

No. Because there is no state hour count to certify, there is no state log form. The driving school certifies the 6 hours of professional training.
