North Carolina Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

60 hours with 10 at night to reach Level 2, then 12 more hours with 6 at night for Level 3 — 72 hours in total.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › North Carolina › Supervised Driving Hours

North Carolina splits its requirement across two levels. Moving from a Level 1 limited learner permit to a Level 2 limited provisional license takes 12 months and 60 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 at night. Moving from Level 2 to a Level 3 full provisional license takes another six months and 12 more hours, including 6 at night. That is 72 hours total — among the highest combined requirements in the country. A parent or guardian signs the NCDMV driving log at each stage.

Who can supervise practice driving in North Carolina

North Carolina is one of the strictest states on who may supervise, and it also ties licensing to school attendance through the Driving Eligibility Certificate.

  • A licensed parent, grandparent, or guardian, or an approved adult designated by them, who has been licensed for at least five years.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • A Driving Eligibility Certificate is required for drivers under 18, certifying school enrollment and adequate progress. No certificate, no license, regardless of hours.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

North Carolina asks for 10 night hours at Level 1 and 6 more at Level 2, which means night driving is not something you finish and forget — it comes back. The NCDMV does not publish a clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness. In Raleigh and Charlotte, that is anything after roughly 5:10 PM in December and 8:30 PM in June, so the cooler months are far more efficient. Level 2 also carries a 9 PM to 5 AM driving restriction, so plan practice for the early evening. Worth doing on purpose: unlit rural two-lanes east of I-95 and mountain roads near Asheville, both of which behave nothing like a lit suburban arterial after dark.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • The log gets checked twice. North Carolina examines it at Level 2 and again at Level 3, so a record that runs out halfway through creates a problem eighteen months later.

How to actually get to 60 hours (then 12 more)

Sixty hours over a 12-month Level 1 period is a bit over an hour a week — comfortable, and the 12-month clock is the real gate. The Level 2 add-on of 12 hours over six months is nothing by comparison, but families forget it exists and then scramble. Put a reminder on the calendar the day Level 2 starts.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does North Carolina require?

60 hours including at least 10 at night to move from Level 1 to Level 2, then 12 more hours including 6 at night to reach Level 3. That is 72 hours in total.

How long does North Carolina's graduated licensing take?

At least 12 months at Level 1 and another six months at Level 2, so about 18 months from the limited learner permit to a full provisional license.

Who can supervise practice driving in North Carolina?

A licensed parent, grandparent, or guardian, or an approved adult they designate, who has been licensed for at least five years, seated in the front passenger seat.

What is a Driving Eligibility Certificate?

North Carolina requires drivers under 18 to present a certificate showing school enrollment and adequate academic progress. Without it, a license will not be issued no matter how many hours are logged.

Does North Carolina have an official driving log form?

Yes. The NCDMV publishes a driving log that the supervising driver signs and that is presented at each level change.
