Wisconsin Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

Supervised practice with at least 10 hours in darkness, documented on the Wisconsin driving log alongside driver education.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Wisconsin › Supervised Driving Hours

Wisconsin requires documented supervised driving practice, including at least 10 hours of driving in darkness, before a teen can get a probationary license. The commonly cited total is 30 hours of behind-the-wheel practice on top of driver education, though you will also see 50 quoted by third-party sites — confirm the current figure with the Wisconsin DMV, because this is one of the numbers most often reported wrong. Driver education is required for drivers under 18, the instruction permit has to be held for at least six months, and the hours are documented on the Wisconsin driving log signed by a parent or guardian.

Who can supervise practice driving in Wisconsin

Wisconsin names several categories of qualifying supervisor, which is more flexible than most states.

  • A parent or guardian who is licensed, or
  • a spouse at least 19 years old, or
  • a licensed driver at least 21 with at least two years of driving experience.
  • A driver education instructor qualifies during course sessions. Confirm the current wording with the DMV before relying on a non-parent supervisor.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Wisconsin words its night requirement as driving in darkness, which is refreshingly literal: not a clock range, just actual dark. Ten hours is roughly twenty half-hour evening drives. Wisconsin's winter makes this easy — Milwaukee and Madison are dark by about 4:20 PM in December, so the drive home from practice already counts, and by January nearly every after-school drive qualifies. In June, sunset is after 8:30 PM and those hours get expensive. Conditions worth practicing in the dark here: lake-effect and squall snow, unlit county roads where deer are a genuine hazard, and the salt-spray freeway where the lane markings disappear.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Winter is not optional in Wisconsin. The state does not require weather hours, but a driver who has never handled ice on a curve has not been taught the thing that matters most here.

How to actually get to the required practice hours

Whichever total applies, the pace that works is a couple of hours a week attached to trips you were making anyway. Wisconsin's driver education requirement means the teen already has structured instruction, so use the supervised hours for the parts a course cannot cover: repetition, bad weather, unfamiliar towns, and the fifteenth time backing out of a tight parking spot.
