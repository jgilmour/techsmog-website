Montana Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night, alongside Montana's traffic education program.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Montana › Supervised Driving Hours

Montana requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen moves from a learner license to a first-year restricted license. Montana's traffic education program is the spine of teen licensing here: teens as young as 14½ can get a Traffic Education Learner License once they are enrolled in an approved course. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Montana's traffic education program uses RoadReady, the supervised driving app from Safe Roads Alliance, so many Montana families are pointed at it directly by their instructor.

Who can supervise practice driving in Montana

Montana's supervision rule is looser on age than most states, which fits a state where a lot of teens are already driving farm equipment. Confirm the current wording with the MVD, since supervision rules get updated.

  • A licensed driver with at least a year of experience in the front passenger seat is the practical standard — verify the current age floor with the MVD.
  • A licensed traffic education instructor qualifies during course sessions.
  • Montana distances change the math. A single trip to a regional center can be two hours of driving. That is real experience, but it is highway experience — make sure town driving, four-way stops, and parking get their share.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night. Montana's latitude produces a dramatic seasonal swing: in Billings, December darkness starts around 4:35 PM, while in late June there is usable light past 9:30 PM. Bank the night hours between October and March and they cost you nothing extra. What is worth practicing in the dark in Montana specifically: unlit two-lane highways where deer and elk cross without warning, long stretches with no reference points other than reflective posts, and the winter combination of blowing snow and oncoming headlights.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Winter driving is the skill that matters here. Montana does not require inclement-weather hours, but ice on a two-lane at highway speed is what new Montana drivers actually face.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the six-month learner period is about two hours a week — and in rural Montana, two hours a week is often a single trip. The trap is that all 50 hours become open-highway miles. Deliberately spend a share of them in Bozeman, Missoula, Billings, or Great Falls, where the traffic, intersections, and parking are the things the road test and the first solo year will actually test.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Montana require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, before a first-year restricted license. A parent or guardian certifies the hours.

Does Montana use RoadReady?

Yes. Montana's traffic education program uses RoadReady, the supervised driving app from Safe Roads Alliance, and many families are directed to it by their instructor. If that is what your program asks for, that is the log to keep.

How old do you have to be to get a Montana learner license?

14 and a half for a Traffic Education Learner License, once enrolled in an approved traffic education course. A regular learner license is available at 15.

How many night hours does Montana require?

10 of the 50. Montana's early winter darkness makes October through March by far the efficient window for collecting them.

Is traffic education required in Montana?

An approved traffic education course is required for teens who start the licensing process before 16. Its behind-the-wheel instruction is separate from the 50 supervised hours.
