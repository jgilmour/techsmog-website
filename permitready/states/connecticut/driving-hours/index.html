Connecticut Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

40 hours of behind-the-wheel practice, an 8-hour safe driving practices course, and a permit period that changes length depending on how you train.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Connecticut › Supervised Driving Hours

Connecticut requires 40 hours of behind-the-wheel practice before a teen under 18 can take the road test. The permit holding period depends on the training route: 120 days if you complete driver education through a commercial driving school or a secondary school program, or 180 days if the family does home training. Everyone under 18 also has to complete the 8-hour Safe Driving Practices course, and a parent or guardian has to attend a 2-hour portion of it. Connecticut does not publish a separate night-hours minimum.

Who can supervise practice driving in Connecticut

Connecticut's supervision rule is stricter than most states' in a way that surprises families: it is not just about age, it is about how long the supervising driver has been licensed and whether their record is clean.

  • A licensed driving instructor, or
  • a person at least 20 years old who has held a valid license for at least four years without suspension, seated in the front passenger seat.
  • The four-year clean-license rule rules out a lot of people — a 21-year-old cousin licensed three years ago does not qualify, and neither does an adult with a suspension in the last four years.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Connecticut does not publish a night-hours minimum, which puts it in a small minority. That is not a reason to skip night practice: Connecticut's own passenger and curfew restrictions for newly licensed teens exist precisely because the 11 PM to 5 AM window is where new drivers get hurt. Aim for 10 hours after dark on your own initiative, and get some of them on unlit two-lane roads rather than only on lit arterials. Connecticut winters make this easy — darkness starts before 4:30 PM in December, so an after-school drive already counts.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Home-training families should keep the tightest records. If your family took the 180-day home training route rather than a commercial school, the log is the only documentation of the 40 hours that exists.

How to actually get to 40 hours

Forty hours over a 120-day permit period is about two and a half hours a week. Over the 180-day home training period it is closer to ninety minutes a week. Neither is heavy. What derails Connecticut families is the sequencing: the 8-hour Safe Driving Practices course has limited seats, and the parent's 2-hour session has to be scheduled too. Book those early, then let the driving hours accumulate around them.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Connecticut require?

40 hours of behind-the-wheel practice for drivers under 18, on top of the required driver education and the 8-hour Safe Driving Practices course.

How long do you have to hold a Connecticut learner's permit?

120 days if you complete driver education at a commercial driving school or through a secondary school program, or 180 days if the family does home training. The difference is the main reason families choose one route over the other.

Does Connecticut require night driving hours?

The DMV does not publish a separate night minimum. Ten hours after dark is a sensible personal target, especially given Connecticut's 11 PM to 5 AM restriction for newly licensed teens.

Who can supervise practice driving in Connecticut?

A licensed driving instructor, or a person at least 20 years old who has held a license for at least four years with no suspensions, seated in the front passenger seat. The four-year clean-record rule disqualifies more relatives than families expect.

Do parents have to take a class in Connecticut?

Yes. A parent or guardian must attend a two-hour session as part of the mandatory 8-hour Safe Driving Practices course for drivers under 18. Seats fill up, so schedule it early.
