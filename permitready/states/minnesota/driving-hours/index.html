Minnesota Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours with 15 at night — or 40 hours with 15 at night if a parent takes the supplemental awareness class.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Minnesota › Supervised Driving Hours

Minnesota requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 15 hours at night, before a teen can apply for a provisional license. The total drops to 40 hours if a parent or guardian completes the state's supplemental parent awareness class — a genuinely worthwhile trade, since the class is short and the discount is 10 hours. The permit has to be held for six months, and drivers under 18 must complete driver education. A parent or guardian certifies the hours.

Who can supervise practice driving in Minnesota

Minnesota's rule is standard, and the parent awareness class is the piece worth acting on early.

  • A licensed driver 21 or older in the seat beside the permit holder.
  • A certified driving instructor or licensed peace officer also qualifies.
  • The parent awareness class cuts 10 hours. It is a supplemental session for parents and guardians, and completing it reduces the requirement from 50 hours to 40. Take it in month one so the target is set before you start counting.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Fifteen night hours is one of the highest requirements in the country, and it does not shrink if you take the parent class — the discount comes off the daytime side. The good news is that Minnesota's winter does most of the work: in the Twin Cities, December darkness starts around 4:35 PM, so any drive after school is a night drive from November through February. In June, sunset is close to 9 PM and night hours are nearly impossible to collect at a reasonable hour. Get them in the winter. Worth doing deliberately: icy roads at night, where black ice and glare combine, and rural highways where snow banks narrow the lane and there is no lighting at all.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Winter is the requirement's real shape. Fifteen night hours in a Minnesota winter takes about a month of ordinary evening driving. Fifteen night hours in a Minnesota summer takes a season.

How to actually get to 50 hours (or 40 with the parent class)

Fifty hours over the six-month permit period is about two hours a week; 40 hours is closer to ninety minutes. Either way the night share is the planning constraint, not the total. The most efficient Minnesota schedule is a permit issued in the fall: the 15 night hours accumulate almost passively through the dark months, and the daytime balance fills in on weekends.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Minnesota require?

50 hours, including at least 15 at night. The total drops to 40 hours, still with 15 at night, if a parent or guardian completes the supplemental parent awareness class.

What is the Minnesota parent awareness class?

A supplemental session for parents and guardians of teen drivers. Completing it reduces the supervised driving requirement from 50 hours to 40. Take it early so you know which target you are driving toward.

How many night hours does Minnesota require?

15, which is among the highest in the country, and the number does not change if a parent takes the awareness class. Minnesota's early winter darkness makes November through February the efficient window.

How long do you have to hold a Minnesota instruction permit?

Six months before applying for a provisional license at 16.

Does Minnesota have an official driving log form?

DVS supplies a supervised driving log, and a parent or guardian certifies the total when the provisional license is applied for.
