Oregon Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

100 hours of supervised driving — or 50 hours plus an approved driver education course. The highest requirement in the country either way.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Oregon › Supervised Driving Hours

Oregon requires 100 hours of supervised driving before a teen under 18 can get a license — or 50 hours plus completion of an ODOT-approved driver education course. That 100-hour figure is the highest in the United States, and the driver education discount is the reason most Oregon families take the course: it cuts the requirement in half. A parent or guardian certifies the hours. The instruction permit has to be held for at least six months.

Who can supervise practice driving in Oregon

Oregon adds a license-tenure requirement on top of the age floor, and the driver education decision is the biggest single lever in the whole process.

  • At least 21 years old and licensed for at least three years, in the front passenger seat.
  • An ODOT-approved driver education instructor qualifies during course sessions.
  • Do the math on driver education early. The course costs money; 50 extra hours of supervised driving costs a parent roughly two months of evenings. Most families conclude the course is the better trade.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Oregon does not publish a separate night-hours minimum, which is surprising for a state that asks for so many total hours. Set your own target of 10 hours after dark, counted between sunset and sunrise. Oregon's winter makes that easy — Portland is dark by about 4:30 PM in December — and its summer makes it nearly impossible at a reasonable hour, with light lingering past 9 PM in June. What is worth practicing after dark here: rain at night on a poorly lit arterial, which is the defining Oregon driving condition; unlit Coast Range and Cascade highways with curves and no shoulder; and the disappearing lane lines on wet asphalt that make Highway 26 miserable in February.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Rain hours are the real Oregon requirement, even though the state does not ask for them. A driver who has only practiced in dry weather has missed most of the year.

How to actually get to 100 hours (or 50 with driver education)

One hundred hours across six months is about four hours a week, every week — the heaviest requirement in the country by a wide margin. Fifty hours is two hours a week, which is ordinary. That gap is the whole argument for the driver education course, and it is why Oregon families should make that decision in the first week of the permit rather than in month four, when the choice is effectively already made.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Oregon require?

100 hours, or 50 hours if the teen completes an ODOT-approved driver education course. Oregon's 100-hour requirement is the highest in the country.

Does driver education reduce Oregon's requirement?

Yes, by half — from 100 hours to 50. That is the largest driver education discount of any state, and it is the main reason Oregon families enroll.

Does Oregon require night driving hours?

The DMV does not publish a separate night minimum. Ten hours after dark is a sensible personal target, and Oregon's rainy winter nights are the conditions most worth practicing in.

How long do you have to hold an Oregon instruction permit?

Six months before applying for a license, for drivers under 18.

Who can supervise practice driving in Oregon?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old who has held a license for at least three years, seated in the front passenger seat.
