South Carolina Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

40 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night, and 180 days on the beginner's permit.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › South Carolina › Supervised Driving Hours

South Carolina requires 40 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can move from a beginner's permit to a conditional or special restricted license. The beginner's permit has to be held for at least 180 days. Drivers under 17 also need to complete an approved driver education course — commonly 30 hours of classroom plus 8 hours behind the wheel — to qualify for a conditional license. A parent or guardian certifies the supervised hours.

Who can supervise practice driving in South Carolina

South Carolina adds an experience requirement to the age floor, which rules out newly licensed young adults.

  • At least 21 years old, licensed, with at least one year of driving experience.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • A driver education instructor qualifies during course sessions.
  • Beginner's permit holders face a daylight restriction in some circumstances — check the current rules so night practice happens within them.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 40 hours have to be at night. The SCDMV does not publish a rigid clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. In Columbia and Charleston, December is dark by about 5:20 PM while June light lasts until nearly 8:30 PM, so winter evenings are the efficient window. South Carolina-specific night driving worth doing on purpose: two-lane roads through the Lowcountry with deer and standing water, I-26 and I-95 at night with heavy truck traffic, and the coastal fog that can drop visibility to almost nothing in spring.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Summer thunderstorm driving is worth catching. Not required, but a July downpour on a road with poor drainage is a condition every South Carolina driver meets, usually alone and usually too soon.

How to actually get to 40 hours

Forty hours over the 180-day permit period is about ninety minutes a week — a light schedule. As usual, the night share is where families come up short: 10 hours after dark is roughly twenty evening drives, and evenings get harder to protect once spring sports start. Put one evening drive a week on the calendar from the first month.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does South Carolina require?

40 hours, including at least 10 at night, before a conditional or special restricted license. A parent or guardian certifies the total.

How long do you have to hold a South Carolina beginner's permit?

180 days. The permit period runs from the issue date regardless of how quickly the 40 hours are finished.

How many night hours does South Carolina require?

10 of the 40. Treat night as actual darkness between sunset and sunrise, and use winter evenings when it is dark before 5:30 PM.

Is driver education required in South Carolina?

An approved driver education course, commonly 30 hours of classroom instruction plus 8 hours behind the wheel, is required for a conditional license before age 17. Those hours are separate from the 40 supervised hours.

Who can supervise practice driving in South Carolina?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old with at least one year of driving experience, seated in the front passenger seat.
