Missouri Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

40 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night — and Missouri is specific about who counts as a "qualified instructor."

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Missouri › Supervised Driving Hours

Missouri requires 40 hours of behind-the-wheel driving instruction, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can move from an instruction permit to an intermediate license. Missouri is unusually specific about who those hours are driven with: the law refers to a "qualified instructor," which means a parent, grandparent, legal guardian, a qualified driving instructor, or a licensed driver at least 25 years old with the parent's permission. The permit has to be held for at least 182 days.

Who can supervise practice driving in Missouri

Missouri's "qualified instructor" definition is broader than most states in one direction and narrower in another: grandparents are named explicitly, but an unrelated adult has to be 25 and have the parent's permission.

  • A parent, grandparent, or legal guardian who is licensed.
  • A qualified driving instructor.
  • A licensed driver at least 25 years old with the parent's or guardian's permission — note the age is 25, not 21.
  • The supervisor rides in the front passenger seat and has to be able to take control.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 40 hours have to be at night. Missouri does not publish a rigid clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. In St. Louis and Kansas City, December gives you full dark by around 5:10 PM and June holds light past 8:40 PM, so the winter months are where these hours come easily. Missouri-specific night conditions worth practicing: rural two-lane highways in the Ozarks with sharp curves and no lighting, and the transition from a lit interstate to an unlit county road, where your eyes need thirty seconds they do not get.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Record who supervised. With the qualified-instructor rule, a log line that names the adult is easier to certify honestly than one that just shows a duration.

How to actually get to 40 hours

Forty hours over the 182-day permit period is about ninety minutes a week. That is a manageable pace and Missouri families rarely miss the total — they miss the night share, because 10 hours after dark takes about twenty evening drives and the evenings fill up fast in spring. Start the night hours in the first month.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Missouri require?

40 hours of behind-the-wheel driving instruction, including at least 10 at night, before an intermediate license. A qualified instructor signs the driving log.

Who counts as a qualified instructor in Missouri?

A parent, grandparent, or legal guardian who is licensed, a qualified driving instructor, or a licensed driver at least 25 years old with the parent's permission. The age-25 rule is stricter than the 21 most states use.

How many night hours does Missouri require?

10 of the 40. Treat night as actual darkness between sunset and sunrise. Winter evenings are the efficient way to collect them.

How long do you have to hold a Missouri instruction permit?

182 days before applying for an intermediate license at 16.

Does Missouri have an official driving log form?

Yes. Missouri requires a signed driving log documenting the 40 hours, and the Department of Revenue publishes a form for it. Bring it when you apply.
