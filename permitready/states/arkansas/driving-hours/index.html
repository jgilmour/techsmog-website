Arkansas Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

Arkansas does not set an hour count. It sets a six-month clean-record permit period instead — which changes how you should plan practice, not whether you need it.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Arkansas › Supervised Driving Hours

Arkansas is one of the few states that does not publish a specific number of supervised driving hours. Instead, the graduated licensing law focuses on time and record: a learner's license at 14, held for at least six months with a clean driving record, before an intermediate license at 16. There is no state hour count to certify and no official log form. That is genuinely different from most states — and it means the only thing standing between a new Arkansas driver and the road test is however much practice the family decides to do.

Who can supervise practice driving in Arkansas

The supervision rule still applies even though the hour count does not.

  • A licensed driver at least 21 years old must be in the front passenger seat whenever a learner's license holder is driving.
  • The clean-record requirement is the real gate. A moving violation or a seat belt citation during the six months can restart the clock, which is a harsher penalty than most states apply.
  • A licensed driving instructor qualifies during a lesson.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Arkansas publishes no night-hours minimum, so there is no number and no official definition of night to work against. Use the national norm as your own target: 10 hours after dark, counted between sunset and sunrise. That is the figure most states landed on independently, and the reason is not bureaucratic — fatal crash rates for 16- and 17-year-olds are dramatically higher at night, and the first few hours of unfamiliar night driving are the ones you want to happen with a parent in the car rather than alone on a county road.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Nobody is checking, so the log is for you. Without a state requirement, a log's only job is to show the family honestly whether the teen has driven in rain, at night, on the highway, and in traffic — or has done forty trips to the same grocery store.

How to actually get to enough practice to actually be ready

With no state number, borrow one: 50 hours with 10 at night is the most common requirement in the country and a reasonable target. Across a six-month permit period that is about two hours a week. The advantage of Arkansas's approach is that nobody is gaming a total — the disadvantage is that it is very easy to arrive at the road test at 16 with fifteen hours of experience and a clean record, which satisfies the state and does not make anyone a safe driver.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Arkansas require?

Arkansas does not publish a fixed statewide hour requirement. The graduated licensing law relies on a six-month learner's license period with a clean driving record instead. Confirm current requirements with Arkansas Driver Services, since states do add hour requirements over time.

Does Arkansas require night driving hours?

No night minimum is published. Most states require around 10 hours after dark, and that is a sensible personal target given how much higher night crash rates are for new drivers.

How old do you have to be to get an Arkansas learner's license?

14. The intermediate license comes at 16, after holding the learner's license for at least six months with a clean driving record.

Does Arkansas have an official driving log form?

No. There is no state supervised driving log form, because there is no hour count to certify.

What happens if you get a ticket during the six months in Arkansas?

The six-month period has to be clean. A moving violation or a seat belt citation can reset the clock, which delays the intermediate license. Confirm the specifics with Driver Services, since how violations are counted can change.
