Maryland Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

60 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night, recorded on Maryland's Rookie Driver Practice Skills Log.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Maryland › Supervised Driving Hours

Maryland requires 60 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can take the skills test for a provisional license. Maryland is one of the states that actually gives you the paperwork: the MVA's Rookie Driver Practice Skills Log is a structured log where practice is recorded against specific skills, not just hours. Drivers under 18 must also complete a certified driver education program (commonly 30 hours classroom plus 6 hours behind the wheel), and hold the learner's permit for nine months.

Who can supervise practice driving in Maryland

Maryland asks for both an age minimum and a license-tenure minimum, which is stricter than most states.

  • At least 21 years old, licensed, and licensed for at least three years.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • A certified driving instructor qualifies during a lesson.
  • Maryland's Practice Skills Log expects a mentor, not just a warm body — the log is organized around skills to teach, which works much better with an adult who is actually coaching.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 60 hours have to be at night. The MVA does not lock down a clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness — after sunset and before sunrise. In Baltimore that means anything after about 4:50 PM in December, and after about 8:35 PM in June. Maryland is also one of the more useful states to do night practice in deliberately, because the driving environments are so different within an hour of each other: the Beltway at night, unlit Eastern Shore two-lanes, and the mountain grades of I-68 out west are three separate skills. Do not do all ten hours in the same suburban loop.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • The Practice Skills Log is organized by skill, not just time. That is a feature: it pushes you to practice merging, parking, and rain driving rather than accumulating repetitive miles.

How to actually get to 60 hours

Sixty hours across the nine-month permit period is about ninety minutes a week — comfortable. The Maryland-specific advice is to use the Practice Skills Log the way it is designed: work through the skill categories rather than driving the same route sixty times. A teen who has logged 60 hours and never merged onto I-95 in traffic has met the number and not the point.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Maryland require?

60 hours, including at least 10 at night, recorded on the MVA's Rookie Driver Practice Skills Log and signed by a parent, guardian, or supervising mentor. Confirm the current night minimum with the MVA before your skills test.

What is the Rookie Driver Practice Skills Log?

Maryland's official practice log, published by the MVA as part of the Rookie Driver program. It is organized around driving skills rather than just hours, so you record what was practiced as well as how long.

How long do you have to hold a Maryland learner's permit?

Nine months for drivers under 18. Applicants 18 and over have a shorter holding period.

Who can supervise practice driving in Maryland?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old who has held a license for at least three years, seated in the front passenger seat. A certified driving instructor also qualifies.

Is driver education required in Maryland?

Yes. A certified driver education program is required, commonly 30 hours of classroom instruction plus 6 hours behind the wheel. Those hours are separate from the 60 supervised hours.
