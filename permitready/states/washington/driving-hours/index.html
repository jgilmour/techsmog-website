Washington Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night — and the supervising driver has to have held a license for five years.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Washington › Supervised Driving Hours

Washington requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, before a teen can get an intermediate license. The supervision rule here is stricter than most: the adult in the passenger seat must be at least 21 and have held a valid license for at least five years. Driver education is required for drivers under 18, and the instruction permit has to be held for at least six months. A parent or guardian certifies the hours when the license is applied for.

Who can supervise practice driving in Washington

The five-year license requirement is the rule people miss, and it disqualifies a lot of otherwise willing adults.

  • At least 21 years old and licensed for at least five years.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • A driver education instructor qualifies during course sessions.
  • A 24-year-old who got licensed at 22 does not qualify. The five-year clock is on the license, not the person.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night. The DOL does not publish a rigid clock definition, so treat night as actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. Washington's latitude makes the seasonal swing severe: Seattle is dark by about 4:20 PM in December and holds light past 9 PM in late June. If the permit period covers a Pacific Northwest winter, the night hours essentially collect themselves. What is worth practicing deliberately: rain at night, which is the defining Washington driving condition and the one where lane markings vanish under headlight glare; unlit two-lanes in the Cascades and on the peninsulas; and passes where snow and dark arrive together.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Wet-road night driving is the local skill. Not a state requirement, but a Washington driver who has never driven in rain after dark has skipped the most common condition they will ever face.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the six-month instruction permit period is about two hours a week. The Washington-specific advice is to check the supervising driver rule before you start counting: if the plan was for an older sibling or a young aunt to do most of the driving, confirm they have had a license for five years, or those hours are not certifiable.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Washington require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, before an intermediate license. A parent or guardian certifies the total when applying.

Who can supervise practice driving in Washington?

A licensed driver at least 21 years old who has held a valid license for at least five years, seated in the front passenger seat. The five-year rule disqualifies many young adults who otherwise meet the age requirement.

How many night hours does Washington require?

10 of the 50. Treat night as actual darkness. Washington's early winter sunsets make November through February the efficient window.

Is driver education required in Washington?

Yes, for drivers under 18. Its behind-the-wheel instruction is separate from the 50 supervised hours.

Does Washington have an official driving log form?

Washington does not collect a page-by-page log. A parent or guardian certifies the 50 hours when the intermediate license is applied for, so keep a dated record you can stand behind.
