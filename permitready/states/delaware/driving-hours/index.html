Delaware Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving with 10 at night — and Delaware is one of the only states that requires the supervising adult to be 25, not 21.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › Delaware › Supervised Driving Hours

Delaware requires 50 hours of supervised driving, including at least 10 hours at night, during the Level 1 learner's permit period. The detail that catches Delaware families off guard: the supervising driver has to be at least 25 years old, not 21 like almost everywhere else. A parent, guardian, or other adult signs a log sheet certifying the hours. Drivers under 18 also have to complete a state-approved driver education course, and the Level 1 permit has to be held for six months before Level 2 driving privileges begin.

Who can supervise practice driving in Delaware

The age floor here is the single most important rule on this page, because getting it wrong invalidates hours you already drove.

  • At least 25 years old and licensed — Delaware sets a higher bar than the 21 most states use.
  • Seated in the front passenger seat for the entire drive.
  • A licensed driving instructor qualifies during a lesson regardless.
  • A 22-year-old sibling does not count in Delaware. If hours were driven with someone under 25, they should not go on the log.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

Ten of the 50 hours have to be at night. Delaware does not publish a strict clock definition, so read night as actual darkness — after sunset, before sunrise. Delaware's Level 1 permit already carries a night driving restriction, so most permit-period night practice is happening in the early evening anyway, which is fine: 6 PM to 8 PM in November and December is fully dark and completely normal driving time. Ten hours is roughly twenty half-hour drives. Get some of them on Route 1 and some on unlit back roads in Kent and Sussex — those are different skills.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Log the supervisor's name. Because of the age-25 rule, a Delaware log that records who was in the passenger seat is easier to defend than one that just records hours.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours over the six-month Level 1 period is about two hours a week. Delaware is small enough that the same twenty-minute errand loop can quietly account for most of it, which is the risk — a driver who has done 50 hours on the same three roads is not really ready. Mix in Route 1 at speed, a Wilmington city drive, and at least a few beach-traffic summer weekends if the timing works.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does Delaware require?

50 hours, including at least 10 at night, during the Level 1 learner's permit period. A parent, guardian, or supervising adult signs a log sheet certifying the hours.

How old does the supervising driver have to be in Delaware?

25. Delaware is one of the few states that sets the supervising driver age above the usual 21. A licensed driving instructor also qualifies. Hours driven with someone under 25 should not be logged.

How many night hours does Delaware require?

10 of the 50. Treat night as actual darkness between sunset and sunrise. Evening drives in November and December are the efficient way to bank them.

Does Delaware have an official driving log form?

Yes. The DMV provides a behind-the-wheel log sheet for certifying the 50 hours. Download the current version rather than reusing an old copy, since forms change.

Is driver education required in Delaware?

Yes, for drivers under 18. A state-approved driver education course is required, and its behind-the-wheel training hours are separate from the 50 supervised hours.
