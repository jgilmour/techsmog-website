North Dakota Supervised Driving Hours Requirements

50 hours of supervised driving in varied conditions — North Dakota asks for range rather than a strict night count.

TechSmog Apps › PermitReady › North Dakota › Supervised Driving Hours

North Dakota requires 50 hours of supervised behind-the-wheel driving experience for teen applicants, and the NDDOT frames it around variety rather than a rigid night count. The state wants practice across different conditions — day and night, different road types, different weather — rather than a single number of night hours. Driver education is required for applicants under 16, and North Dakota has one of the youngest permit ages in the country at 14. A parent or guardian certifies the hours.

Who can supervise practice driving in North Dakota

Because North Dakota permits start at 14, a lot of practice happens with a very young driver on high-speed rural roads. Confirm the current supervision rule with NDDOT before you rely on it.

  • A licensed adult in the front passenger seat for every drive — in practice, a parent, guardian, or an adult they approve.
  • A driver education instructor qualifies during course sessions.
  • Permits at 14 mean two full years of practice are possible. Use them: North Dakota's requirement is modest, and the winter driving skills that matter here take more than one season to build.

Night hours, and what "night" means here

North Dakota does not publish a separate night-hours number, but its requirement is written around variety, and night is explicitly part of what "varied conditions" means. Use 10 hours after dark as your own target, counted between sunset and sunrise. North Dakota's winter does most of the work: in Bismarck, December darkness starts around 5 PM, and by January any drive after school is a night drive. The conditions worth seeking out here are specific and serious: blowing snow at night, where the headlights light up the snow and nothing else; unlit two-lanes with no shoulder; and ground blizzards that erase the road in seconds.

What counts as a loggable hour

  • Public-road driving with a qualified supervisor counts. The trip to school, errands, highway miles, practice loops around the neighborhood — it all counts as long as the permit holder is at the wheel and the supervising driver is in the front passenger seat.
  • Time parked, idling, or waiting in a drive-through does not count. Log driving time, not trip time.
  • Empty parking lots and closed courses generally do not count. The point of the requirement is real traffic.
  • Hours before the permit was issued do not count. The permit has to be in hand first.
  • Log the conditions, not just the clock. Since North Dakota's requirement is about variety, an entry that says "snow, gravel, dusk" is more faithful to the rule than one that just says forty minutes.

How to actually get to 50 hours

Fifty hours is about two hours a week over six months, and North Dakota's permit age of 14 means most families have far longer than that. Use the extra time on purpose: spread practice across a full winter so the ice and snow hours are real, and mix in Fargo or Bismarck traffic if you live rurally. A driver who has only ever driven straight, empty roads is not ready for a four-lane with merging traffic.

Frequently asked questions

How many supervised driving hours does North Dakota require?

50 hours of supervised behind-the-wheel experience, with NDDOT emphasizing practice in a variety of conditions rather than a fixed night-hours count. Confirm the current requirement with NDDOT.

Does North Dakota require night driving hours?

NDDOT does not publish a separate night minimum, but night driving is part of the varied conditions the requirement is built around. Ten hours after dark is a sensible personal target.

How old do you have to be to get a North Dakota instruction permit?

14, which is among the youngest permit ages in the country. Driver education is required for applicants under 16.

Does North Dakota have an official driving log form?

NDDOT and driver education providers supply a log form for certifying the 50 hours. Get the current version rather than reusing an old copy.

What conditions should North Dakota teens practice in?

Night, gravel, snow, ice, and blowing snow, plus at least some city traffic if you live rurally. The state's requirement is written around variety precisely because winter driving here is the hard part.
